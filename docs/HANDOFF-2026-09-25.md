- **HAC bandwidth.** `scripts/metrics.py:343-344` hardcodes
  `lags=min(5, len(returns)-1)` and never sees the label horizon `h`. Labels are
  overlapping h-period returns, inducing MA(h−1) serial correlation, so the

### → ChatGPT: dividend-accounting review, no repo access

Pure quantitative-accounting review. You have no repository access and need none.
Answer the question on its merits and show your reasoning.

**Setup.** A single-operator daily-bar equity backtester maintains a funded ledger:
real cash balance, integer share positions, next-open fills, commission and
slippage applied. It trades a fixed five-ticker US mega-cap universe over 10 years.
Prices are **unadjusted** historical dollars; splits and dividends are held in a
separate corporate-actions table and applied to the ledger as events.

**Constraint.** Only free data is available. The price/actions source provides
dividend **ex-dates** but not historical **payment dates**. No free source provides
10 years of historical payment dates. So the payment date must be *declared*:

`pay_date = ex_date + declared_lag`

where `declared_lag` is one constant per dataset, calibrated as the maximum
observed ex→pay lag in the only free window where payment dates are available
(~1 year), and applied to all 10 years.

**Answer these five, precisely:**

1. Does crediting dividend cash at `ex_date + declared_lag` rather than the true
   payment date bias the ledger's reported P&L, Sharpe, or maximum drawdown? State
   the sign of each bias and the mechanism. Distinguish effects that vanish over a
   long horizon from those that accumulate.
2. Is a **deliberately long** lag genuinely conservative, or does it introduce a
   different error? Cash credited late understates available cash and may suppress
   a buy the strategy would have made. Does that suppression bias results
   pessimistically in all cases, or can it flatter results in some?
3. **The buy-and-hold baseline** is a mandatory comparator in this project. Does
   applying the same declared lag to the baseline preserve comparability, or does
   an asymmetry arise because the baseline never trades on dividend cash while the
   strategy might?
4. Should dividend cash be assumed **reinvested** or held idle? Name the convention
   a systematic desk would use for a total-return comparison, and what changes in
   the reported numbers under each.
5. What magnitude of `declared_lag` is defensible for US large-cap equities, and
   what is the empirical distribution of ex-date→pay-date lag? If you are not
   confident in the distribution, say so plainly rather than estimating — an
   invented number would be written into a manifest and treated as calibrated.

**Rejected alternative, for context — tell me if the rejection is wrong.**
Excluding dividends from ledger cash entirely was considered. It is conservative
for the strategy's cash balance but understates total return, and it makes the
buy-and-hold baseline wrong in the direction that flatters every strategy compared
against it. Is that reasoning sound?

Answer as a quant risk reviewer. Where you are uncertain, say so explicitly — this
output feeds a spec that gets implemented.
